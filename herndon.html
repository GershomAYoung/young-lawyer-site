The court

Herndon has its own traffic and misdemeanor docket.

The Fairfax County General District Court states that traffic violations and criminal misdemeanors under the local laws of the Town of Herndon are heard at a courthouse within Herndon. Fairfax County General District Court judges sit there, and Fairfax County clerks staff the court.

That makes Herndon simultaneously local and county-wide: a town police department and municipal code feeding into Virginia’s district-court system.

Local history

Why is it called Herndon?

The community developed around the railroad in the mid-nineteenth century. It took the name Herndon in honor of Commander William Lewis Herndon, a Virginia-born U.S. naval officer and explorer. He died in 1857 while commanding the steamship Central America, remaining with the ship as it sank after helping passengers into lifeboats.

The railroad depot that became the center of town dates to about 1857 and survives today as a museum operated by the Herndon Historical Society.

1850s

Railroad village

The railroad helped turn a rural crossroads into a recognizable community. The depot became the town’s physical and commercial center.

1863

Mosby’s Herndon Station raid

During the Civil War, Confederate partisan John S. Mosby led a raid at Herndon Station, part of the town’s unusually dense Civil War history.

1879

Incorporation

Herndon incorporated as a town. Because it is a Virginia town rather than an independent city, it remains within Fairfax County.

1917

The great fire

A major fire destroyed much of downtown Herndon, reshaping the commercial center.

1968

The last train

Rail service through the old depot ended. The former rail corridor later became part of the Washington & Old Dominion Trail.

Police & local government

A municipal police department inside Fairfax County.

The Herndon Police Department is a town agency, distinct from the Fairfax County Police Department. That is possible because Herndon is an incorporated town with its own municipal government even though it remains geographically and politically within Fairfax County.

For current police information, records, programs, and official contacts, use the Herndon Police Department ↗. Historical materials are also available through the town and the Herndon Historical Society.
