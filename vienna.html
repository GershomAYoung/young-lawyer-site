The court

Vienna cases are heard in Vienna.

Fairfax County General District Court explains that traffic violations and criminal misdemeanors under the local laws of the Town of Vienna are heard at a courthouse within Vienna. Fairfax County General District Court judges sit on the town docket and Fairfax County clerks staff the court.

The case

Traffic consequences are cumulative.

The ticket is only the beginning of the analysis. Depending on the charge and circumstances, a disposition can implicate DMV demerit points, a driving record, insurance, licensing, court costs, and the time required to resolve the matter.

Local history

From Ayr Hill to Vienna.

The settlement was once known as Ayr Hill, after a prominent local house. In 1854, the community adopted the name Vienna. The railroad soon made the town a transportation and commercial center.

The Freeman Store and Museum, built in 1859 near the railroad, survives as one of Vienna’s defining historic landmarks and is operated with the Historic Vienna organization.

A new name

The Freeman Store

The store opened beside the railroad and later served a remarkable range of community functions. It remains a museum and local-history center.

1890

Incorporation

Vienna incorporated as a town. Like Herndon, it remained within Fairfax County rather than becoming an independent city.

Early automobile era

12 miles per hour

Vienna’s own historical materials recount an early local automobile speed limit of 12 miles per hour—a useful reminder that traffic regulation has been municipal business for nearly as long as automobiles have been on local roads.

Town government

Vienna is in Fairfax County, but it is not merely a neighborhood.

As an incorporated Virginia town, Vienna has its own elected government and municipal police department while remaining part of Fairfax County. Virginia’s independent cities operate differently: they are separate from counties.

The Vienna Police Department ↗ describes a long tradition of community-oriented policing. The town’s historical resources document the evolution of public safety alongside Vienna’s growth.
